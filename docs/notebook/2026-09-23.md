## Results / numbers
- FK vs MuJoCo over 1000 random poses inside the joint limits: max position error **3.3e-16 m**,
  max rotation error 1.3e-15. Every link frame matches, not just the tip.
- DH table derived and verified to **2.6e-15 m** against our FK:

  | row | joint | θ offset | d | a | α |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | shoulder_pan | 0° | 0 | 0.0304 | +90° |
  | 2 | shoulder_lift | −76.032° | −0.01828 | 0.1160 | 0° |
  | 3 | elbow_flex | +73.825° | 0 | 0.1350 | 0° |
  | 4 | wrist_flex | −87.793° | +0.01810 | 0 | +90° |
  | 5 | wrist_roll | +178.792° | −0.15923 | 0 | 0° |

- Random search as "IK": pointing the gripper down is easy (0.02° in 1000 samples), but hitting a
  position *and* a direction is hopeless — 111 000 samples still 21 mm off.
- `pytest`: 15 passed.

## Problems & how I solved them
- My first DH attempt gave garbage (residuals ~1, errors ~2 m). Cause: I built frame *i*'s x axis
  from the common normal to the **next** axis. The rule is the normal from the **previous** axis,
  with the origin on axis *i*. After fixing that, all residuals dropped to 1e-16.
- The `T_to_dh` residual turned out to be the useful debugging tool: a transform that can't be
  written as Rz·Tz·Tx·Rx means the frames are wrong, and it says so immediately.

## What I learned
- Columns of a rotation matrix = the child frame's axes in the parent frame.
- Homogeneous coordinates exist so that rotate-plus-translate composes by plain multiplication.
- DH = 4 numbers per joint, bought by giving up the freedom to place link frames.
- Row 4 has a = 0: the wrist pitch and roll axes intersect → kinematic decoupling, which makes IK
  easier.
- d₂ = −1.83 cm and d₄ = +1.81 cm nearly cancel, which is why the tip sits almost in the arm plane.
