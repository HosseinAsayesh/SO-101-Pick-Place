# 2026-09-24 — Lesson 03: inverse kinematics, workspace, and a height ceiling

**Phase:** 2 · **Time spent:** —

## Goal for this session
- Solve IK in closed form for top-down grasps, map the workspace, and learn the general
  (Jacobian / damped least squares) method.

## What I did
- Self-check quiz on lessons 01–02: 4.5/5. The gap was the inverse of a 4×4 transform — it is
  [Rᵀ, −Rᵀt], not just Rᵀ.
- Wrote `src/so101/ik.py`: `TopDownIK` (closed form) and `dls_ik` (numerical), plus
  `geometric_jacobian` and `pose_error`.
- Wrote `scripts/05_inverse_kinematics.py` (all branches, round-trip check, workspace map,
  height sweep, figures) and `tests/test_ik.py` (10 tests).

## Results / numbers
- Closed-form IK: 0.0 nm position error, 2.4e-6 deg angle error over 500 random targets, ~0.7 ms
  per call including all 8 branches.
- For the cube at (0.22, 0.08) yaw 30°: 8 solutions, **1** inside the joint limits
  (q = [−24.2, 11.1, 11.7, 67.2, 8.6]°, front/elbow-up).
- Workspace at 1.5 cm height: 1490 cm² reachable at some jaw angle, 1287 cm² at every jaw angle,
  max reach ≈ 31 cm. The cube is reachable at all 6 yaw angles tested.
- **Height ceiling: the gripper can only hover pointing down up to ~9 cm** (8.7 cm above the cube).
  `wrist_flex` (±95°) runs out. At z = 10 cm the top-down workspace is completely empty.
- DLS reproduces the closed-form solution to 0.001° but takes 6 ms instead of 0.7 ms.
- Jacobian at the grasp pose: condition number 26, smallest singular value 0.068 (healthy).
- `pytest`: 25 passed.

## Problems & how I solved them
- First version applied the "jaws are symmetric every 180°" alternative by just adding π to the
  roll. Wrong by 15.8 mm, because the tip sits 7.9 mm off the roll axis — rotating 180° swings it
  across. Fixed by treating ψ and ψ+180° as two separate targets, each with its own wrist centre.
- Elbow up/down labels were inverted for the "arm swung backwards" branches: the in-plane basis
  flips when the plane rotates past 90°. Now the label is computed from world geometry (is the
  elbow above the shoulder→wrist line?).
- `damping=0` in DLS raises `LinAlgError`. Not a bug: with 5 joints, J Jᵀ (6×6) has rank ≤ 5, so
  it is singular by construction. The damping term is what makes the solve possible at all.

## What I learned
- Kinematic decoupling: because the wrist axes intersect, the wrist centre depends only on joints
  1–4, which splits a 5-unknown problem into four small ones.
- A sin q + B cos q = C collapses to one sine; it has two solutions, or none if |C| > √(A²+B²).
- Law of cosines gives the elbow angle; cos is even, hence exactly two elbow branches.
- Check the triangle inequality rather than clipping arccos — clipping hides "unreachable".
- The Jacobian's columns are just zᵢ × (p_tip − pᵢ) and zᵢ.

# 2026-09-24 (later) — Lesson 04: trajectories, control, first pick-and-place

**Phase:** 3 · **Time spent:** —

## Goal for this session
- Turn IK poses into smooth motion, close the gripper on the cube, and measure a baseline
  success rate with the cube's pose known exactly.

## What I did
- `src/so101/trajectory.py`: cubic/quintic time scaling, joint-space, hold and Cartesian segments
  (with IK branch continuity), `Trajectory`.
- `src/so101/pick_place.py`: 9-segment state machine, servo playback with logging, the success
  rule from the brief, `pad_gap` / `jaw_angle_for_gap`, `set_cube`, `run_trial`.
- `tools/derive_grasp_model.py`: generates `models/so101/so101_pick_place.xml` (upstream model +
  collision pads). `scene.xml` now includes the derived model.
- `scripts/06_pick_and_place.py`, `tests/test_pick_place.py` (14 new tests), lesson 04.
- Also made `scripts/05_inverse_kinematics.py` faster: 1 cm default grid, progress line, `--fine`.

## Results / numbers
- One run: 10.5 s of simulated motion computed in **0.2 s** (50x real time).
- Servo tracking: peak **0.13°** during the transfer, settled 0.02–0.03° at every hold.
- Gripper: pads 0.27 cm apart at 0°, 4.33 cm at 55°. For a 3 cm cube: open 52°, close 23°.
- **Baseline success rate: 25/25 = 100% (95% Wilson CI 87–100%)**, mean placement error 2.1 mm,
  worst 3.4 mm, with the cube pose known exactly.
- `pytest`: 39 passed.

## Problems & how I solved them (four real bugs)
1. **Convex hulls.** MuJoCo collides meshes as convex hulls; the fixed finger's mesh wraps around
   the hinge, so its hull filled the jaw opening and the gripper behaved as a solid block — the
   cube was swept aside every time. Fix: disable the mesh collisions on the two gripper bodies and
   add explicit box pads on the real inner faces.
2. **Grasp frame ≠ IK frame.** `gripperframe` is at the wrist output, 3 cm from the jaw centre.
   Added `GRASP_OFFSET = (-0.030, 0, 0.014)` and `TopDownIK.grasp`. It also broke the ψ+180°
   symmetry from lesson 03 — the grasp point is off the roll axis, so each yaw needs its own tip
   target; before the fix the cube was placed 2.8 cm off.
3. **Pad below the table.** The pads reached 1 cm past the fingertip point, so they pressed into
   the table at grasp height; `shoulder_lift` saturated at 3.35 N·m and the arm hung 1 cm high.
   Shortening the pads to 3 cm took tracking error from 3.3° to 0.13° and placement from 6.9 mm
   to 1.1 mm.
4. **Wedge effect.** The jaw is angular, so flat pads are parallel at exactly one angle; at others
   they wedge the cube out. Pre-rotated the moving pad by 26°, the holding angle for a 3 cm object.

## What I learned
- Quintic time scaling = zero velocity AND zero acceleration at the ends; the cost is a 1.875x
  peak speed. Acceleration steps become torque steps, which make held objects slip.
- Cartesian segments matter where the path shape matters (descend/lift); joint segments elsewhere.
  Pick the IK branch nearest the previous knot or the arm flips posture mid-grasp.
- A PD servo's error under a smooth reference is mostly velocity lag; torque saturation is a wall
  that no gain fixes.
- A position-controlled gripper needs a commanded width: measure gap vs jaw angle and invert it.
- Debug physics by measuring the geometry (probe maps, contact lists), not by tuning constants.
